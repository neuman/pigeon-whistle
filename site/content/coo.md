The whistle must sound where the coo of a city pigeon is loudest: 340 Hz, rising to 470 Hz.

## The two notes in the model

With the finger hole closed, the closed form gives 340.09 Hz. The potential-flow solve gives 340.12 Hz at the limit of seven grids. With the hole open, they give 469.63 Hz and 469.77 Hz.

The limit is 10 cents from each target (C1, C2). The closed form is 0.45 cents from the low target and 1.37 cents from the high target. The evaluators also run the solve on one grid, and it stays inside the limit.

The closed form has three end factors, and they are fitted to the solve of this one geometry. C3 holds the two to each other within 1 %. The worst of four states is 0.25 %. This shows that the two models agree. It does not show that either is right about a printed whistle.

The chamber is 81.9 mm long. That is 0.111 of the wavelength of the open note, and the limit is 0.125 (C7). The plain law for a small chamber is trusted up to 0.10, so the design is past that point. The solve carries the first correction, and C3 keeps the closed form with it.

## The trim

A first print can miss by about 10 %, which is 165 cents. The recordings tolerate 8 %, which is 133 cents. The vent on the rump covers part of the difference.

- Plug the vent with wax: the closed note falls 89.8 cents, to 322.93 Hz.
- Ream the vent to 3.5 mm: the closed note rises 88.7 cents in the solve, to 358 Hz.

The closed form gives 77.6 cents for the ream. C4 uses that lower number, and its limit is 50 cents each way.

The trim brings a miss of about 5 % back to the target. It brings a miss of 12.6 % on the flat side, or 13.7 % on the sharp side, back inside the 8 % band. A miss of 15 % stays outside the band after the trim. Such a print needs a new print with a corrected window.

## The jet

The model assumes a breath of 200 Pa. The jet then leaves the windway at 18.4 m/s. The Strouhal number is 0.13 on the closed note and 0.18 on the open note. A recorder feeds its note between 0.07 and 0.27 (C5, C6).

The band comes from recorders, and the pressure is an assumption (C31). Inside the band, the jet can feed the note. Only a built part shows that the whistle speaks.

## The window and the chamber

The window opens straight into the chamber. The chamber section beside the jet is 5.73 times the window area, and the limit is 4.5 (C9). Two concepts of the design panel had a throat behind the window, and the panel ranked them as the quiet ones. No model here predicts loudness.

## What this board does not show

The person printed the first whistle and played it on video (input first-print-test). At a soft breath both notes sit close to the model. A harder breath raises them well above it, because the jet pushes the note up and the model leaves the jet out. No evaluator settles a played note: the person records C21 and C22.

- A printed whistle must sound between 313 and 367 Hz with the hole closed, after the trim (C21).
- It must sound between 432 and 508 Hz with the hole open (C22).
- A sound level meter must read between 60 and 90 decibels, A-weighted, at half a metre (C26).

The low target is the choice of the person. The independent analysis of the recordings supports 470 Hz. It reads the start of the loudest partial at 380 Hz, with quartiles of 363 Hz and 413 Hz. In that recording, 3 % of the loudest energy lies close to 340 Hz. The evidence comes from 2 birds at most.

Three assumptions stand under this board, and the person has not recorded them. Loud enough means heard across a room (C29). Sound travels at 346 m/s in the chamber (C30). The breath is 200 Pa (C31). C1 and C2 flip if the speed of sound is 10 % lower.
