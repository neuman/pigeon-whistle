## 2026-11-20 — Match the loudest part of the coo

`match-the-loudest-part-of-the-coo`

The two notes sit where the coo is loudest, as the person asked in the interview (R2): 340 Hz with the finger hole closed, rising to 470 Hz with it open, 5.6 semitones.

**Rejected**

- **the deep fundamental of the coo, about 170 Hz rising to 250 Hz** — the person asked for the loudest part, not the deepest (R2); at half the frequency the same window needs four times the chamber, about 475 cm^3, which is no palm-size bird (R4), or a neck about 30 mm deep by the pitch law, and the acoustics notes already place 340 Hz at the small-chamber limit — infeasible · returns if (not machine-checkable) "when the bird may be about four times the volume, which is not palm size (R4)" · source: input coo-pitch-analysis
- **a low note of 380 Hz** — the independent re-measurement of the recordings reads the low end of the loudest partial at 380 Hz (quartiles 363 to 413), and only 3 %, 11.5 % and 13 % of the loudest-partial energy lies within 25 Hz of 340 Hz; the person chose 340 rising to 470 in the interview. 380 to 470 is 3.7 semitones, at the low edge of the rises measured (3.7 to 5.1 semitones), and 340 to 470 is 5.6, above them — preferred against, naming neuman — nopekit why match-the-loudest-part-of-the-coo says whether they recorded it · returns if (not machine-checkable) "when neuman hears a printed whistle beside the recordings and wants the low note higher: reaming the vent to 3.5 mm reaches about 358 Hz in the model, and 380 Hz needs a reprint with a larger window" · source: input coo-pitch-analysis

**Params:** `target_closed_hz`, `target_open_hz`  
**Claims:** `C1`, `C2`, `C21`, `C22`  
**Evidence:** `inputs/measurements/coo-pitch-analysis.md`

How strong the evidence is: the analysis supports 470 Hz (a third of the loudest-partial energy of one Paris recording lies within 25 Hz of it) and reads the low note nearer 380 Hz than 340 Hz. It rests on two effective birds: one identified pigeon in Paris, 40 usable notes over two recordings, and four notes from Sangli. The two recordings of the same bird disagree on which partial is loudest. The analysis justifies no tolerance tighter than 8 % on either note, and that is the band of C21 and C22. Nothing has been printed or blown: a first print can miss by about 10 % either way, and a miss to the sharp side moves toward what the re-measurement reads.

## 2026-11-20 — The window opens straight into a rump lobe

`the-window-opens-straight-into-a-rump-lobe`

The chamber runs back into the rump as a lobe 15 mm wide, and the window in the left flank opens straight into that air: 253 mm^2 of chamber beside the jet, 5.7 times the window. The windway is a straight 30 mm slot through the tail and the lips stop 15 mm short of the window.

**Rejected**

- **a window behind a throat (the print-first and character-first concepts)** — window paths 15.8 and 17.0 mm long, 3.7 and 4.3 equal-area radii, outside every recipe in the acoustics notes: quiet and breathy by the panel's estimate; their designers' pitch models read high, and an independent solve gave 317 and 331 Hz against a designed 340 Hz — dominated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "when the chamber has to shrink below about 100 cm^3: a throat then works as a neck, if its junction is in the model and it is 7.5 mm wide or more" · source: input vessel-flute-acoustics-notes
- **a long skewed windway to a 21 x 17 mm scoop on the flank (the acoustics-first concept)** — the scoop lies where the thumb goes (64 cents flat with a pad resting over it) and a fingertip reaches the labium inside it; the 43.5 mm slot has about twice the length in which a string or a seam can sit; as drawn the concept failed the overhang evaluator (0.71 to 0.82 % against 0.5 %) and its print estimate was about 21 h and 107 g — dominated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "when a guard rib keeps fingers off the scoop and its roof is drawn again at 42 degrees: its pitch model was the only one of the three an independent solve confirmed"

**Params:** `lobe_width_mm`, `lobe_ridge_z_mm`, `windway_length_mm`, `lip_stop_x_mm`  
**Claims:** `C9`, `C10`  
**Evidence:** `model/pigeon.py`

The lobe is the narrowest the design allows (250 mm^2 and 4.5 times the window). It was taken that narrow on purpose: the window's own conductance was 16 % too high for 340 Hz, and a narrower lobe lowers it without narrowing the jet. C9 holds it from becoming a throat again.

## 2026-11-20 — A tuning vent printed open

`a-tuning-vent-printed-open`

A 2.1 mm vent on the crown of the rump is part of the whistle as printed. Plug it with wax and the closed note falls 90 cents; ream it to 3.5 mm and the note rises 89 cents in the solve (78 cents by the closed form). One print can be trimmed down or up.

**Rejected**

- **a blind pilot, drilled out after printing** — a one-sided trim: it can only raise the note, and published instruments miss the closed form in both directions, so a first print can land sharp as well as flat — dominated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "when measured prints of this whistle land flat every time" · source: input vessel-flute-acoustics-notes
- **no vent** — the closed note then rests on the window alone, the part the model predicts worst (10 % typical on published instruments); the trims left are filing the labium back and pouring wax into the chamber, and neither is undone — dominated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "when one measured print has fixed the window's end factor and the next print lands inside the 8 % band untrimmed" · source: input vessel-flute-acoustics-notes
- **a 1.5 mm horizontal vent in the chest skirt (the character-first concept)** — a finger can cover it there (about 146 cents flat in the panel's review) and a 1.5 mm horizontal hole may print shut — dominated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "when the crown of the rump is needed for something else; the vent then goes back to a place the hand does not reach"

**Params:** `vent_d_mm`, `vent_x_mm`, `vent_ream_max_mm`  
**Claims:** `C4`, `C21`  
**Evidence:** `model/solve.py`

What the vent does not cover: a first print can miss by about 10 %, 165 cents, and the vent reaches 89 or 90 cents each way. It brings a miss of up to about 5 % back to 340 Hz, and a miss of up to about 13 % back inside the 8 % band. A miss of 15 to 20 % means a reprint with a corrected window.

## 2026-11-20 — Solve the pitch, then calibrate the closed form

`solve-the-pitch-then-calibrate-the-closed-form`

The two notes come from a potential-flow solve of the drawn geometry, taken to zero grid size over seven grids; the closed form in the model carries three end factors calibrated on that solve and is held to it within 1 % in all four states (C3).

**Rejected**

- **the textbook closed form alone, with end factors from the books** — in the design panel the two concepts whose pitch models were not checked against an independent solve read 7 % and 3 % flat of one (317 Hz and 331 Hz against a designed 340 Hz): one closed form left out the junction between window and throat, the other designer's solver read 5 to 6 % high in conductance on its own test case; the published instruments in the acoustics notes put an uncalibrated window at 10 % typical, and the vent here needs an end factor of 4.69 where the books give 1.46 to 1.70 — dominated · returns if (not machine-checkable) "when one printed whistle has been measured and the window's end factor is backed out of that measurement: the acoustics notes expect about 3 % from the closed form then" · source: input vessel-flute-acoustics-notes
- **one grid of the solve, read as it is** — every single grid reads flat of the limit, by 3.0 % at 0.8 mm, 2.3 % at 0.6 mm and 0.8 % at 0.25 mm on the closed note: the convergence is first order at the sharp labium edge, so a design centred on one grid is off by 14 to 52 cents — infeasible · returns if C1 limit >= 52

**Params:** `k_window`, `k_hole`, `k_vent`  
**Claims:** `C1`, `C2`, `C3`, `C7`  
**Evidence:** `model/solve.py`, `model/acoustics.py`

What this does not buy: both models give the passive resonance of the geometry as drawn. The limit of the grid study carries about 0.3 % in frequency, and one 0.6 mm grid taken to zero about 0.4 %. The jet, viscous loss, the lips, the temperature and the print are outside both models. The three end factors belong to this geometry and do not transfer: the reamed-vent reach by the closed form is 11 cents short of the solve.
